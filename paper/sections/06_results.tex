\subsection{Interpretation}
\label{sec:res-interpretation}

\emph{The five paragraphs below were written before the run was executed and before any result
existed --- four on 2026-08-10, the trigger and this preamble on 2026-08-11. Exactly one is
retained; the other four are deleted rather than softened.}

\textbf{Four of the five branches are selected by the manifest. The fifth is a ruling made against
it, and we say so rather than implying an automatic that does not exist.} The verdict emitter
defines exactly three outcome words --- \textsc{survives}, \textsc{null} and
\textsc{halt\_adjudicate} --- and the primary outcome is a total binary function of the clauses:
\textsc{survives} if none fails, \textsc{null} otherwise, with a guard superseding to
\textsc{halt\_adjudicate}. \textbf{No manifest can contain the word \textsc{inconclusive}.} It is
an adjudication outcome, consistent with the pre-registration committing the design to an
inconclusive response when five seeds cannot separate the arms, and it is reached by a person
reading the manifest, not emitted by it.
% artifact: src/trikaal/eval/verdict.py:75--77 (SURVIVES, NULL, HALT_ADJUDICATE — three words);
%   :1103 `primary = SURVIVES if not failing else NULL`; guards supersede at :1268/:1331.
%   `grep -rn INCONCLUSIVE src/` returns nothing.

That distinction has a consequence we are obliged to close rather than note. If the manifest reads
\textsc{null} and nobody adjudicates, we publish a null for a result that did not resolve --- the
error this section exists to prevent --- and a branch with no trigger is a check that cannot fire.
We therefore fix the trigger in advance, in terms readable off the manifest without judgement.

\paragraph{Trigger for the \textsc{inconclusive} adjudication.} Where
\texttt{verdict.primary} is \textsc{null}, the \textsc{inconclusive} branch is \emph{obliged} ---
not merely permitted --- if any of the following holds.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item \texttt{clauses.1\_paired\_ci.pass} is true while \texttt{clauses.2\_mde\_paired.pass} is
    false. The confidence interval excludes zero --- the effect's sign is established --- while its
    magnitude falls below what the design declared in advance it could resolve. That is an
    underpowered measurement, not an absent effect.
  \item \texttt{power\_guard.checks.*.worst\_within\_cell\_ir\_range} is at least
    \texttt{clauses.2\_mde\_paired.mde\_paired}. The across-seed spread of the statistic equals or
    exceeds the smallest effect the design claims to detect. Because that minimum detectable effect
    is built from scoring noise alone and carries no training-variance term
    (\cref{sec:lim-power}), this state means the power claim rests on an incomplete noise model,
    and a null computed under it cannot be read as absence.
  \item \texttt{dual\_specification.agree} is false. The two pre-registered specifications, run on
    the same artifacts, disagree on the outcome word. Which answer one believes then depends on
    which specification one believes, which is what unresolved means.
\end{enumerate}

The condition is sufficient, not necessary: a \textsc{null} meeting none of the three is reported
as a null. Two candidates were considered and rejected. \texttt{verdict.double\_null} and
\texttt{verdict.fallback} concern the quantizer axis --- whether $\IR(2)-\IR(1)$ is claimable under
the identical rule --- and a clean two-arm negative is a null, not an unresolved measurement. And a
sub-endpoint reading of the degeneracy guard's activity leg was rejected because that leg is
endpoint-only by ruling (\cref{app:repro} D.2); using a sub-endpoint threshold here would smuggle
in the band the ruling declined to adopt. We also declined to express clause~2 as a fraction of the
claimed effect, because that would require inventing a constant; the form above compares two
quantities the manifest already carries.

%   power_guard.checks.<name>.worst_within_cell_ir_range; verdict.{double_null, fallback}.

\paragraph{If the legibility gate fails on real data.} This is the mechanism's own modal
prediction, and it is a result rather than an accident. The gate requires at least $0.90$ sign
accuracy on every one of the six live microstructure dimensions, recovered from each bar's own
token; a hard stop means the re-specified interface, which restores that property on an
entropy-calibrated fixture, does not restore it on real microstructure. What that establishes is
the first non-fixture confirmation of capacity eviction: on real data, at the pinned budget, a
reconstruction-trained tokenizer does not make microstructure legible per-bar even when built to.
The claim ends there. It does \emph{not} establish that no tokenizer can --- $\lambda$ was fitted
on a synthetic proxy (\cref{sec:lim-matching}), the budget is one point in a space we did not
search, and a gate failure is uninformative about how far short the interface fell. It also does
not test the microstructure hypothesis at all: no second-stage training is authorized, so cells 1
through 5 are never scored and no verdict is emitted. The pre-registered adjudication executes ---
the gate raises rather than degrading, the failure is recorded with its per-dimension values, and
the decision to re-specify, re-tune or stop is made against the recorded numbers rather than
retrospectively.

\paragraph{If the outcome is \textsc{survives}.} A positive $\Delta\IR(4-5)$ that clears the
paired confidence interval, the minimum detectable effect, the economic floor and the deflation
benchmark establishes that real trade-flow imbalance carries information beyond input capacity, and
that a tokenizer built to preserve it delivers that information to a cost-aware trading decision.
The claim ends there, and three limits bind it immediately. It is not a calibrated estimate of how
much: \cref{sec:lim-placebo} states that $\Delta\IR(4-5)$ mixes information with an unquantified
capacity handicap, so the measured difference is an upper bound on the information effect rather
than an estimate of it. It is not evidence that our BSQ baseline is competitive, so the
quantizer-axis marginals remain reported rather than claimed. And it is one exchange, one venue,
one instrument class and one year of scored data, with no regime-transfer test --- a positive
result here is an existence proof on this slice, not a property of markets. The mechanism of
\cref{sec:mechanism} would remain a fixture result; a positive outcome is consistent with it and
does not confirm it, because many differences between cells 2 and 4 could produce the same sign.

\paragraph{If the outcome is \textsc{null}.} A null is the outcome the design's own arithmetic
makes most likely, and we pre-committed it as publishable before any result existed, so it is
reported without reframing. It means that under this pre-registered decision rule, at this budget,
on this slice, the microstructure arm did not beat its placebo by a margin the design could
resolve. It does \emph{not} mean microstructure carries no information: the minimum detectable
effect is $3.518$ annualized IR against a prior of $|\text{RankIC}| = 0.027$, so the design would
fail to detect a real effect of the size we actually expect, and \cref{sec:lim-power} states that
the effect we can detect is larger than the effect we predict. Nor does it falsify the mechanism of
\cref{sec:mechanism}, which is established on a fixture and is not contingent on this outcome. What
a null does establish is narrower and still worth publishing: that the pipeline, with the interface
defect removed and the legibility gate passed, does not convert this microstructure signal into net
information ratio at this scale. Given that the published record in adjacent fields is measurably
skewed toward positive findings --- \citet{franco2014publication} track a population of studies
from design to publication and find null results systematically less likely to be written up at all
--- a pre-registered null with its power analysis stated in advance is a contribution to the record
rather than a failed experiment. \Cref{sec:lim-power} names what would change the answer: more
scored data, a lower-cost venue, or an effect larger than our own prior.

\paragraph{If the outcome is \textsc{inconclusive}.} \textsc{inconclusive} is a distinct outcome
from \textsc{null}, and conflating them would be the more comfortable error. A null says the effect
was not there to the resolution we committed to. \textsc{inconclusive} says the instrument did not
resolve the question either way --- the clauses did not separate, or they separated in a pattern
the pre-registered rule does not map to a decision. The correct report is that the experiment did
not answer its question, not that the answer was negative. We name in advance what we will not do:
we will not re-run with more seeds to break the tie, because the seed count was fixed at five up
front precisely so that this response is unavailable, and we will not promote a secondary
diagnostic to a headline. \Cref{sec:lim-power} carries the reason an inconclusive outcome is
foreseeable rather than surprising.

\paragraph{If a guard emits \textsc{halt\_adjudicate}.} Neither guard can change an outcome; each
can only withhold one. A halt means the verdict's own inputs cannot support a verdict --- a cell's
book is constant-direction, or a cell's across-seed range reaches the size of the effect being
claimed. What happens next is procedural rather than interpretive: the halting cell and the guard
that fired are reported with their per-seed values, no primary outcome is stated, and the clause
results are published unchanged, since the guards are computed after the clauses and do not touch
them. A halt is not a null and must not be reported as one.
