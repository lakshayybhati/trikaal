\subsection*{F.1\quad Status of the item that was blocked}

The ruling on the degeneracy guard's activity leg has been made: \textbf{the leg is not banded},
recorded in \texttt{docs/degeneracy\_guard\_activity\_leg\_RULING.md} and carried as
pre-registration amendment tag v1.6.28. Appendix~D.2 now records the leg as endpoint-only
\emph{by ruling} rather than by omission, with the three compensating disclosures the ruling
requires; \cref{sec:lim-filter} states what it leaves uncovered.

\textbf{The tag now exists, and the sequence is worth recording because the discipline is the
point.} The ruling named itself amendment v1.6.28 while that tag was absent from the log:
\texttt{grep -c v1.6.28 docs/m6\_prereg.md} returned zero, and the highest tag present was
v1.6.27. For as long as that held, this paper reported the log as carrying $41$ tags --- what the
log \emph{contained} --- and cited the ruling by document rather than by tag, because a count
written to an assertion about a log rather than to the log is exactly the failure the log exists
to prevent. The entry has since been written (\S7 v1.6.28, dated 2026-08-11, citing commit
\texttt{2012acc}), and only then did the count move to $42$ in \cref{sec:repro-prereg} and
Appendix~D.4.

Two facts about the log that a reader checking this should have. The $58$ counts \emph{distinct
revision tags}, which is what the paper claims; the log carries $55$ top-level entries, because
three tags --- v1.0, v1.6.24 and v1.6.27 --- appear as in-body references without headers of their
own. And one claim inside the ruling document does not survive checking: it states that the
compute-optimal token figure was ``verified from \texttt{PINNED\_STEPS\_STAGE2} at HEAD'', but that
pin supplies only the step count. The figure needs three inputs, and the batch size of $32$ is a
dataclass default rather than a conformance pin, so it can drift where a pin cannot. The
arithmetic holds for the denominator it used, and that denominator turned out to be the wrong
one: $20.00$ tokens per parameter is computed against the backbone excluding the multi-token
heads, and against the realized predictor the budget is $13.40$ (\cref{sec:lim-filter}). The
incomplete sourcing is corrected in the log entry rather than left in the side document.

\subsection*{F.4\quad Claims we could not trace, and checks that misreported}

\textbf{The warm-up figures are reconciled by category, and the residual arithmetic is not.} The
ingest record describes a ``$\sim$800-bar z-score warm-up'' while the pinned constants give $180$
bars fast, $720$ slow and $1{,}440$ for the volatility scale, and the measured drop is $809$ bars
of $279{,}930$ on the thinnest instrument. The apparent conflict --- a drop smaller than the
largest constant --- dissolves on inspection of what the constants do: warm-ups \emph{mask} rather
than delete. They set mask bits and invalidate targets; the bars remain in the lake. The $809$ is a
count of rows dropped to warm-up and quality control, which is a different quantity from a mask
length, and the same record separately reports $1{,}363$ micro bars masked on that instrument. What
we have \emph{not} reconciled is the exact per-symbol arithmetic relating segment count, mask
length and dropped rows, and we do not attempt it here. \Cref{sec:data-causal} quotes only the
measured drop; no number in the paper depends on the residual.
% artifact: src/trikaal/data/features.py:180 `m[s0:s1, idx] = np.maximum(m[s0:s1, idx], warm)` and
%   :200--201, :209 — warm-ups write MASK bits and invalidate targets; they do not delete rows.

\textbf{Six of the twenty-one bindings in Appendix~D.5 were wrong as first written}, and
the error is more serious than a transcription slip because D.5's own claim is that each slot has
exactly one defensible source. Five clause outcomes named \texttt{.passed} where the emitter writes
\texttt{.pass}; the power-guard rows named fields the emitter does not define; and the legibility
gate was given a verdict-manifest path when it is a separate Stage-1 receipt that no verdict
manifest contains. All twenty-one were re-verified against the emitter and the table corrected. An
external spot-check of ten bindings had reported nine resolving, which is consistent with the error
rate and is the reason we re-checked all of them rather than the two we were pointed at.

\textbf{Three verification checks misreported during these passes, and all three were the check's
fault.} A bibliography-versus-PDF checker reported an entry missing, when the entry renders
correctly and the checker's surname extraction had broken on a \LaTeX{} accent escape. A watermark
check reported Figure 8 unwatermarked, when the watermark is present and legible and
\texttt{pdftotext} fragments rotated glyphs. And an appendix-presence check reported two appendices
missing, when it had been given titles the document never used. Each was resolved by looking at the
artifact; none was reported as a finding. They are recorded because a probe's own defect presenting
as a finding is the failure mode this project has hit most often.

\textbf{One layout defect was found by looking at a render rather than by any check:} Figure 8's
outcome box overran its panel. Fixed and re-verified visually.

\textbf{Coverage of the claims audit is partial and is not represented otherwise.} An external
spot-check covered six numbers, not spread across the $54$ artifact paths, and all six reconciled
exactly. The remainder of \cref{app:claims} is \emph{unverified by independent check}, not passed.

\subsection*{F.5\quad What the run changes}

\emph{This entry listed what a completed run would change. The run is over --- the gate stopped it
--- so the list is settled rather than pending, and it is kept in that form because what it
predicted is a fact about how contingent the paper actually was.}

Four of the five items are done: four interpretation branches were deleted, one title and abstract
variant was selected and locked, \cref{sec:results} carries the gate receipt in place of a verdict,
and \cref{fig:legibility} was re-rendered from the gate's own artifact. \textbf{One item was
withdrawn rather than done, and it was never possible.} It read ``re-rendering Figs 8--10 from the
artifact with \texttt{-{}-real}'', describing a flag that the generator does not implement --- it
has no argument handling at all --- and that could not have run in any case, because under the
pre-registration's stopping rule no verdict manifest is ever emitted, so the flag has no input that
can exist. The documentation of it is the defect, and it is removed rather than implemented.

\textbf{One item remains, and it is a mechanism rather than an intention.} This appendix must
not ship, and ``we will remember to remove it'' is the shape of defect the rest of this paper spent
two days closing. The submission target is \texttt{paper/submission.tex}, which sets a build flag
that omits this appendix; \texttt{main.tex} keeps it and remains the internal read-through build.
\texttt{paper/check\_submission\_build.py} compiles both and asserts the difference in \emph{both}
directions --- that the submission build lacks this appendix, that the internal build still has it,
that every other section survives, and that the submission build is strictly shorter --- because a
one-sided check passes just as happily if the appendix is deleted outright or the gate is inverted.
It has been observed to fail under each of those three mutations, with a semantic no-op as a
negative control. Nothing else was contingent on the outcome.
