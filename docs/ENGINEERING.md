# Trikaal — engineering guide

Project overview, the non-negotiable invariants, and the engineering norms this repository is built to. Every norm below was produced by a specific failure, which is named beside it.

## What this is

**Trikaal** — a **TOKENIZER STUDY**, not a foundation model. The object under study is the *tokenizer* for **crypto 1-minute K-lines**; the built-from-scratch decoder-only model (**31,795,200 realized params** at the FSQ vocab (891,1225); the **BSQ arms realize 31,725,568** — 69,632 fewer, **0.219% of the full model**, an artifact of vocabulary-dependent embedding/head rows, §7 v1.6.17/v1.6.34). **QUOTE THE REALIZED TOTAL: it is what a reader gets when they load the published checkpoint.** The **backbone excluding the MTP heads** is 21,301,248 / 21,231,616 — a NAMED SECONDARY, never the model size; the MTP heads are 10,493,952 params (33.03% of the artifact) and are identical across arms, which is why C-19's gap is 69,632 either way but 0.219% rather than the superseded 0.327% is the **measurement vehicle** — held fixed and matched across every ablation arm so the only varied factor is the quantizer × input arm. Released as a research artifact (code + weights on HuggingFace + a paper + a live demo). It is a *controlled* evolution of **Kronos** (Shi et al. 2025, arXiv:2508.02739) with exactly **one** novel research contribution: a **microstructure-aware FSQ tokenizer** for financial K-lines. Calling it a foundation model overstates the artifact and misdescribes what the ablation measures — do not reintroduce that framing.

**Current phase: implementation underway.** M1 (synthetic vertical slice) and M2 (BTCUSDT single-symbol real slice + Stage-1 tokenizer) are complete; M3 (Stage-2 AR) is next. The live build order and per-milestone exit gates live in `docs/ROADMAP.md`. The authoritative *design* remains the blueprint spec — build against it, not against assumptions or against Kronos's code.

## Non-negotiable invariants

These are the things that are easy to get wrong and that would sink either the paper or the model. Do not violate them without explicit user sign-off:

1. **TFI, never OFI.** Microstructure imbalance here is computed from free Binance aggTrades = signed *executed-volume* imbalance = **trade-flow imbalance (TFI)**. True order-flow imbalance (OFI) needs orderbook depth and is explicit **v2** future work. Use the name `TFI` in all code, configs, and prose — a reviewer who sees "OFI" computed from trades alone rejects on the technicality.
2. **Causal-safety / no lookahead is a hard invariant.** Every feature, normalization statistic, volatility scale, and prediction target for bar *t* may use **only** data known by the close of bar *t*. This is enforced by a first-class CI unit test — never weaken, skip, or `xfail` it.
3. **One headline claim.** The single contribution is the microstructure-aware FSQ tokenizer. MTP heads, volatility-scaled targets, and the eval harness are *secondary engineering*, not competing research claims. Resist scope sprawl into a second claim.
4. **The 2×2 ablation is the proof:** {BSQ, FSQ} × {OHLCV-only, +microstructure} at matched params/bits, **plus a required shuffled-microstructure placebo (Cell 5)** that separates microstructure *information* from input *capacity* — if the +micro gain does not exceed the placebo gain, the microstructure leg is withdrawn. Cell 1 (BSQ + OHLCV-only) is **our** BSQ baseline at matched bits-per-token, and it is **NOT externally validated** — the G-§8.C.3 Kronos gate was found **unexecutable** and **dropped as binding** (Lakshay, 2026-08-03; prereg §7 v1.6.22): the paper publishes **two** Kronos_small RankICs **2.4× apart**, both on **Shanghai Stock Exchange 15-minute equity** bars — a market and frequency v1 firewalls out — and running the weights needs Kronos model code invariant 8 forbids. **No Kronos weights are ever pulled.** The compensating control is a **required disclosure** carried in every verdict manifest and refused if absent: *"we cannot exclude that our BSQ baseline is weaker than a reference BSQ implementation, which would inflate the FSQ-vs-BSQ comparison reported in §5."* **This paragraph said "externally validated" for a month after the gate was dropped — a false statement of what validation we performed, in the file every session reads first.** Keep the AR backbone matched to the Kronos_small dims (8 layers, d_model 512, d_ff 1024, 8 heads) so the comparison stays clean.
5. **Headline metric = cost-aware net Information Ratio** under a forecast-magnitude execution filter, with a 0.1–0.3% per-trade transaction-cost model. IC / RankIC / MAE / R² are *secondary* diagnostics. Purged walk-forward + embargo is mandatory, never optional.
6. **FSQ levels are derived empirically** from reconstruction fidelity; fairness vs BSQ is controlled by **bits-per-token**, NOT by forcing a 2²⁰ vocab. Keep the coarse→fine hierarchy but monitor per-level reconstruction contribution and watch for residual-magnitude decay (mitigation: per-stage learnable scaling).
7. **Determinism is a deliverable, not hygiene (scoped honestly).** Data pipeline, frozen-stats, and prediction replay are **bit-exact** from one config file + a pinned seed + content-hashed inputs; **GPU training is bit-exact only under the deterministic-attention fallback** (FlashAttention-2 is otherwise non-deterministic) and every run records its mode. Every produced dataset is content-hashed so any past prediction can be reconstructed.
8. **Full in-house independence.** No Kronos code or weights are ever part of Trikaal — every component (attention, blocks, tokenizer, FSQ, training loop, eval, metrics) is self-written. Kronos's paper is cited prior art; **its weights are never pulled and appear nowhere.** The eval harness was specified to load them as an external validation target for our BSQ baseline, but that gate is **dropped as binding** and unexecuted (invariant 4, prereg §7 v1.6.22) — `external_validation.GATE_IS_BINDING = False`. The sentence that used to end here described a validation we did not perform. Infrastructure libraries (PyTorch, FlashAttention, DuckDB, …) remain fair game — infrastructure, not research content.

## Architecture (intended, two-stage — mirrors Kronos)

1. **Tokenizer** (`trikaal/tokenizer/`): a small Transformer autoencoder encodes the per-bar **16-dim microstructure vector, of which 13 are live** (OHLC-shape + log-volume/amount + TFI/trade-flow; **funding and open-interest occupy dims 13–15 but are constant-zero and masked on 100% of bars in the v1 lake** — specified and wired, structurally absent, §7 v1.6.17) → **FSQ** quantizer (per-dimension levels, grouped into a coarse subtoken + a fine subtoken) → decoder. Trained with a hierarchical reconstruction loss (`L_coarse + L_fine`); **no commitment loss** (FSQ does not need one).
2. **AR backbone + MTP** (`trikaal/model/`): decoder-only causal Transformer (**31,795,200 params realized at the FSQ vocab; 31,725,568 at the BSQ vocab** — the totals INCLUDING the MTP heads, i.e. what the shipped checkpoint contains; backbone-excluding-MTP is 21,301,248 / 21,231,616 as a named secondary. The "~27M" in older text was the *target*, never the built artifact — quote the realized TOTAL, and pinned in `tests/model/test_predictor.py`) over token sequences (≤512 bars). RoPE, RMSNorm, Pre-LN, learnable temporal embeddings (minute/hour/day-of-week/day-of-month/month). Hierarchical subtoken prediction (coarse softmax → fine via intra-block cross-attention conditioned on the *sampled* coarse subtoken). DeepSeek-V3-style **MTP** heads (a causal chain, not independent parallel heads) provide multi-horizon (1/5/15/60-min) distributional output; Monte-Carlo trajectory sampling remains available for full distributions.

Supporting subsystems, each first-class:
- **Data pipeline** (`trikaal/data/`): Binance CSV dumps (klines + aggTrades) + futures API (funding, OI — **specified, NOT ingested in v1; those dims are masked everywhere**) → immutable raw → **Parquet** partitioned by `(symbol, frequency, date)` → **DuckDB** query layer. Includes ingest+inference data-quality gates and dataset content-hashing.
- **Eval harness** (`trikaal/eval/`): co-equal with the model — purged walk-forward + embargo, the 2×2 ablation runner, the cost-aware net-IR backtest, and the secondary diagnostic metrics.

The **per-bar feature vector is the method foundation** — every downstream component depends on its exact definition and causal rules. Read the feature spec section first.

## Tooling & commands (the standard to wire up; repo is pre-code)

- Lint / format: `ruff check .` and `ruff format .`
- Tests: `pytest` (full suite); single test: `pytest tests/<file>::<test_name> -q`
- Pre-commit hooks + GitHub Actions CI running tests on push; the causal-safety/lookahead test is a **required CI gate**.
- **Control-arm rule (standing, prereg §7 v1.4.7):** a probe or gate **must refuse to emit a verdict when its own control arm fails**. If the treatment and the control fail alike, the correct output is `PROBE INVALID` — never a conclusion about the treatment. A probe must also distinguish the failure signature it is testing for from an unrelated fault. This is the same class of defect as an exit code masked by a pipe (see the pipefail rider): a harness that hides its own failure and lets an unverified claim through.
- **Fixture-discrimination rule (standing, prereg §7 v1.5):** a **mutation or negative check must first prove that its own fixture can discriminate the cases it claims to separate**. An assertion that would pass just as well against a gate that does nothing is not a check. Concretely: the v1.5 A.4 mutation check ("the conformance gate rejects the superseded all-arms `var_sr` basis") could not fire, because the conformance fixture's trial values were a modular function that made the placebo-only and all-arms dispersions **numerically identical** — the assertion passed vacuously. It was caught only because the mutation test **failed to fail**. The fix is the norm: the fixture is now cell-dependent and the test asserts `_var_all_arms(full) != _var0(full)` **before** relying on it (`tests/eval/test_conformance.py`).
- **The three are one defect in three costumes — "a check that cannot fail is not a check":** the **pipefail rider** (a `| grep | tail` masked an exit-1 and let a false anchor claim into a commit message; stated in `docs/m6_prereg.md` §7 v1.4.1, cross-referenced above), the **control-arm rule** (a probe emitted a verdict while both arms died of the same `TypeError`), and the **fixture-discrimination rule** (a mutation assertion that no gate could have failed). Each hides a harness's own failure and lets an unverified claim through. Treat any new gate, probe, or negative test as suspect until it has been shown capable of failing.
- **Degeneracy rule (standing, prereg §7 v1.6.10) — A PERFECT AGREEMENT SCORE IS AS SUSPICIOUS AS A FAILING ONE.** Any agreement, correlation, or match statistic must carry a degeneracy check on its own inputs *before* its value is read. A collapsed input scores perfectly for free. Concretely: in the C-1 arm-symmetry experiment, `micro_shuffled/pre_v1_4` scored a **perfect 1.0000 sign agreement with `variance_ratio = 0.000`** — the single-bar decode had collapsed to a CONSTANT, whose sign trivially agrees wherever the reference sign is also constant, while |Δ| was **1.246× the in-window sd**. Without the flag the primary comparison would have read `|diff| = 0.1338 → ASYMMETRIC` **on the basis of an artifact**. This is the project's own pathology — the frac_neg lock, activity pinned at 0/1 — reappearing *inside a metric* rather than inside a result. Sign agreement is never read without the variance ratio beside it.
- **Self-scaling rule (standing, prereg §7 v1.6.10) — DO NOT INVENT A BAND; SCALE AGAINST THE CLAIM OR AGAINST THE STATISTIC'S OWN SPREAD.** When asking whether a difference is material, express it relative to the effect being claimed, or to the across-seed spread of the statistic that produced it — the shape `verdict.power_guard` already uses when it refuses to report a ΔIR smaller than its own inputs' wobble. A difference inside its own measurement noise is not a measured difference. My first C-1 symmetry reading used a hand-chosen 0.05 band and the value landed at 0.0469, i.e. the conclusion rested on a convention I had invented; it was replaced with the self-scaling form.
- **Baseline rule (standing, prereg §7 v1.6.3) — WHEN CHECKING A CLAIM ABOUT A FILE, CHECK IT AGAINST THE COMMIT THE CLAIM WAS MADE ABOUT.** A *distinct* member of the family above: not a check that cannot fail, but a check run against the **wrong baseline** — which looks identical from the inside, because the check does fail, honestly, on the file you actually read. Line numbers drift under our own fixes. Concretely: an audit cited a false rule string at `verdict.py:519-521`; the builder read the current file, found `_per_seed_delta` there, and reported the audit wrong. The builder's own C-2 fix had added 64 lines (758 → 822), moving the string from 520 to 584 — `git show <audited-commit>:<path> | sed -n '520p'` returns it verbatim. The correction was withdrawn. Before disputing any line-referenced claim, `git show` the commit it was made against.
- **Class rule (standing, prereg §7 v1.6.13) — FIX THE CLASS, NOT THE INSTANCE. When a defect is found at a named site, sweep every site of that class before closing it.** A pass that corrects exactly the lines an audit pointed at leaves its siblings standing and closes the finding, which is worse than not having looked — the finding now reads as resolved. Two instances, both from the C-17 pass itself: (1) §3 clause 5's own body still described the superseded all-arms 180-value `var_sr` basis, and the C-9 fix had corrected `N = 180 → 60` **two lines above it and stopped** — both halves of one sentence, one edit apart; (2) `verdict.enumerate_dsr_trials`'s docstring read *"5 × 3 × 3 × 4 = 180"* and survived the C-17 pass **that corrected this very file's module docstring**. Both were found only by building a sweep over the whole class (`scripts/m6_manifest_prose_sweep.py`) and running it — neither was visible from re-reading the fix. The corollary: **when the class is large enough to need a tool, the tool is the deliverable, not the individual fix** — and the tool itself is suspect until it has been shown capable of failing (this one silently skipped `3x` and `1.5x`, the two recipe numbers most worth checking, until its own extractor was mutation-tested). **And once, in the test that was supposed to catch it:** `test_gate_skips_masked_everywhere_dims` carried the docstring *"SKIPPED with a named receipt entry, never trivially passed"* and a body asserting only `"skipped" in receipt["per_dim"]["8"]` at `min_acc=0.0` — **it never read `receipt["pass"]`**, so it passed for years while the gate returned `pass: True` on exactly that input. The defect was not an oversight; **it was the written contract, and its guard was a check that could not fail.** So the class to sweep is never only the production code: the unit test asserting a behaviour is itself a site of the same defect, and "a test exists for it" is not evidence until you have read what the test actually asserts.
- **Render rule (standing, 2026-08-17) — A FIGURE IS NOT VERIFIED UNTIL IT HAS BEEN RENDERED AND LOOKED AT.** Coverage, assertions and code review establish that a figure is **constructed**; they say nothing about what it **shows**. Paint order and coordinates are invisible to every test we have. This is the execution rule applied to OUTPUT rather than to code paths — "never been executed" becomes "never been *seen*" — and it is the third time this project has been saved by looking at the thing instead of at the description of it. Concretely, in the figure the CSV dashboard had been shipping: (1) the seed legend was drawn at y=508 while the disclosure box spanned 492–578 and was appended **afterwards**, so the box painted over it and the figure had **no legend at all**; (2) SVG `<text>` does not wrap, so the 210-character over-dispersion caveat — *the single qualifier named as non-negotiable* — ran to x≈1178 in a 1020-wide viewBox and ended mid-word at "…vs the returns it forecast", **cut in half by the image whose entire job is to carry it**; (3) the forecast occupied 2.8% of the plot width, so the subject of the figure was invisible. 818 tests were green, the code had been reviewed, and neither the builder nor the supervisor could see any of it, because both read the code and never rendered the picture. The fix for the wrap is the model for this class: **budget the text arithmetically rather than by eye** — a monospace face advances exactly 0.6em per character, so a character budget computed from the box width is a *proof* that the line fits, where a proportional face is a guess that was wrong by 180px. And the corollary for anything drawn: **assert the geometry, and select elements by `id` rather than by a shape heuristic** — two of these tests picked "the rect with `rx=6`", and when a second such rect was added one silently began measuring the wrong box and still passed.
  - **★ COROLLARY (added 2026-08-17, from the second occurrence in one session) — FIGURE GEOMETRY IS COUPLED, AND NO TEST SEES THE COUPLING. ANY change to it requires a RE-RENDER AND A RE-MEASURE OF EVERY TEXT NODE, not only of the element that changed.** Widening the y-axis gutter from 78px to 112px — to hold a price label instead of a normalized ratio — narrowed the disclosure box that derives its width from the same `pl`/`pw`, and pushed a subtitle line past the right edge: **the identical clipping class as the over-dispersion caveat above, reintroduced by a change that had nothing to do with it.** So *"I changed the axis"* is never a statement about the axis; it is a statement about **every box whose width is derived from it**. And the standing instrument is **`getBBox()` on the RENDERED artifact, not your own arithmetic** — the arithmetic is what produced the layout, so re-deriving the layout from it is the shared-input rule with both arms in one head. The browser's own font metrics are the independent second arm, and they are what caught this, twice: once at 983.2px inside a 984px box (0.8px of headroom, exactly at the limit and therefore fragile across faces), and once at 1199.5px inside a 1120px viewBox.
- **Tooling rule (standing, 2026-08-15) — THE CODE YOU WRITE TO MANAGE THE WORK IS LESS RELIABLE THAN THE WORK, AND IT IS LESS RELIABLE PRECISELY BECAUSE IT IS EXEMPT FROM THE DISCIPLINE THE WORK GETS.** Monitors, mutation harnesses, formatters, cleanup greps, dry-run caps, cost estimators: written fast, run once, never tested, never reviewed — and then trusted absolutely, because their output is what everyone reads *instead of* looking at the thing itself. **Four instances, three of which corrupted or misreported the very work they were managing:** (1) the **monitors** — false `SHARD_0_FAILED`, false `BOX_GONE`, false P1 death, 40 blind minutes, and a poller that watched `actual_status` while the box said `intended_status: stopped` in a field it never read: *four instrument failures, zero failures of the thing instrumented*; (2) the **mutation harness** that threw `substring not found`, never applied its mutation, and reported `MUTANT_EXIT=0` as though the test had survived — **a false green from the instrument whose entire job is to prove a check can fail**; (3) the **formatter**: a line-splitting script written to fix `E501` warnings broke a Python string literal and left `m6_csv_dashboard.py` unparseable, which was only recoverable because the file was untracked and could be rewritten from scratch; (4) the **dry-run cap** that truncated the RESULT instead of the WORK, so a "2 symbols × 8 decisions" rehearsal scored all 35,063 decisions and timed out — *a rehearsal that costs what the real thing costs rehearses nothing.* The rule has two halves. **A monitor's output is a hypothesis; the box is the evidence** — every real conclusion in this project came from reading the subject directly. And **a tool that MODIFIES the work must be verified like the work**: before trusting a harness's pass, assert that its mutation actually landed; before trusting a formatter, re-parse the file. The generalization is the point — this is not "my monitoring is flaky", it is that *management code is production code with the tests removed*.
- **Shared-input rule (standing, 2026-08-14) — AN AGREEMENT TEST CANNOT DETECT AN ERROR ITS TWO ARMS SHARE. A control that shares the treatment's input is not a control.** This is the control-arm rule generalized from *failure* to *input*: that rule says a probe must refuse a verdict when its own control fails; this one says a probe proves nothing about any input both arms read. Concretely: the demo's acceptance gate compares the demo's assembly against the production assembly and passed **9/9 bit-identical** — while both arms called `trikaal.data.lake.connect()`, which defaults to `processed/bars`: **ONE symbol, 2023 only, 525,600 bars**. The money run scored `processed/universe_bars` — 200 symbols, **2,103,840 bars each**. The demo was serving forecasts computed on a normalization history **4× shorter** than production's, and the gate agreed with itself the whole time. Nothing was mocked and no check was skipped; the two arms simply shared a wrong input, so no disagreement was *possible*. **It was caught by rendering a SECOND SYMBOL — by WIDENING THE INPUT, not by tightening the check** — and no amount of additional assertion on the existing arms would ever have found it. So: enumerate what your arms share (data source, config default, clock, RNG, environment) and vary it, because a shared input is invisible from inside the comparison. The corollary is a design rule: **pin the data source explicitly rather than inheriting a library default**, since a default is exactly the kind of input both arms inherit silently.
- **Mock rule (standing, prereg §7 v1.6.26) — A MOCK IS NOT A SUBJECT. ASSERT THE CALL, NOT THE STUB'S REPLY.** A test that replaces the thing it is verifying and then checks only what the replacement returns has verified the replacement. Concretely: the first F3 test stubbed `subprocess.run` with `lambda *a, **k: _Ok()` and asserted `_driver_version() == "580.159.03"`; corrupting the production argv to `--query-gpu=NOTHING` left it **green**, because the stub answers correctly no matter what it is asked. **The argv IS the contract** — a real `nvidia-smi` given the wrong field prints an error and the lookup falls back to the placeholder, which is exactly the defect. So record the arguments the code passes and assert on those. This was caught by `scripts/m6_reaudit_mutations.py` reporting `★ NOT CLOSED`, an instrument that did not exist eight days ago and **has now caught its own author twice** — which is the argument for building the harness rather than the individual checks.
- **Execution rule (standing, prereg §7 v1.6.25) — "HAS NEVER BEEN EXECUTED" IS NOT AN OPERATIONAL STATE. IT IS AN UNMEASURED RISK, AND EXECUTION IS THE ONLY INSTRUMENT THAT MEASURES IT.** Coverage, review and a green suite establish that code EXISTS and that the paths someone thought of behave; they say nothing about the paths nobody thought of, and they cannot distinguish "correct" from "never run". Concretely, twice in one pass and the pair is the whole lesson: the **R1 codebook gate** was verified by confirming the SYMBOL existed, not that the GATE FIRED — it sat below a `return` and had never executed; the **R5 money driver** was verified by confirming it was BUILT, not that it RAN — its `score_cell` full return dropped two REQUIRED disclosures, so it could never write a single eval artifact, and **657 tests could not see it** because the end-to-end test asserted on a sibling field and the driver had never been run to completion at that configuration. Both are verifying EXISTENCE instead of BEHAVIOUR. The corollary is operational: **any component whose first execution is on rented hardware is an untested component, whatever its coverage says** — which is why `docs/m6_fanout_runbook.md` §1a orders P1 (local `--dry-run` at the shipped HEAD, $0) and P2 (one box, reduced scale, forced determinism inherited into EVAL) ahead of the fan-out, and why finding a defect there is the gate working rather than a delay.
- **Remediation rule (standing, prereg §7 v1.6.25) — AUDIT THE FIX, NOT ONLY THE FINDING. A GATE IS CLOSED ONLY WHEN SOMETHING HAS WATCHED IT FAIL.** The code written to close an audit finding is the least-reviewed code in the project: it is written fast, by the person whose blind spots produced the defect, while everyone else is busy verifying the *report*. Concretely: the v1.6.22 codebook gate — the fourth instance of "specified but not enforced", and the compensating control for dropping the C-4 external gate — shipped **below its function's `return bad`** and was unreachable from the moment it was written; 601 tests stayed green because none fed it an artifact it was meant to reject. It was verified by **grepping for `PINNED_CODEBOOK_MIN_UTILIZATION` and finding it** — confirming a symbol exists is not confirming a gate fires. So a finding is closed only when its PRE-FIX source has been restored in an isolated tree and the new test has been observed to FAIL there (`scripts/m6_reaudit_mutations.py`), and that harness needs its own **negative control** (a semantic no-op that must still pass), because "9/9 CLOSED" is otherwise a check that has never been seen to fail.
- **Reporting rule (standing, prereg §7 v1.6.24) — AN OMITTED CLAIM IS HARDER TO CATCH THAN A FALSE ONE. State every routine verification's EXIT CODE explicitly, pass or fail.** A report that silently drops a line it has always carried reads as clean, because a missing green line must be *noticed* while a wrong one only has to be *read*. Concretely: the v1.6.23 report omitted its `ruff` line and `ruff check` was **exit 1** — six errors in files that same commit created. Nothing false was stated. The symmetric failure on the reviewing side is the pipefail rider: an exit code read from `ruff … | head` is `head`'s. **A verification you did not actually see is not a verification** — so `ruff` and the Gate-A anchor are quoted the same way, by exit code, from an unpiped command.
- **Context-stripping rule (standing, 2026-08-10) — AN ARTIFACT MUST REMAIN SAFE WHEN ITS CONTEXT IS STRIPPED. Assume every excerpt travels alone, and design it to be read that way.** A caveat that lives *outside* the thing it qualifies is not protection: a figure gets cropped, a panel gets screenshotted into a talk, a table gets pasted into a slide, a caption gets quoted without its section, a number gets lifted out of a manifest. In every one of those the qualifier is what gets dropped and the value is what survives. Concretely: Figures 9 and 10 of the paper drew placeholder cost-stress and seed-dispersion panels at plausible positive information ratios **with the microstructure arm above the OHLCV arm above the placebo** — the exact ordering the pre-registered run exists to test — protected only by a DRAFT watermark that any crop removes. Nothing false was written; the *figure* was false, and the file that made it said so in a header no reader of the PDF ever sees. The rule is therefore constructive, not annotative: **placeholder data must be impossible to mistake for a result even with the watermark removed** — absent values, absurd magnitudes, or symmetric about zero — and the same standard binds captions, tables, abstracts, receipt fields and any excerpt that can be quoted on its own. This is the sibling of the reporting rule: that one says a *missing* claim is harder to catch than a false one; this one says a *detached* qualifier is no qualifier at all. Applies to the paper and to every artifact we emit, not only to figures.

## Out of scope for v1 (firewalled — do not add)

Equities / cross-asset data, Bybit/OKX sources, orderbook depth (→ true OFI), base-class (~100M) scale-up, MLA attention, and architecture-level latency micro-optimization (a serving concern, handled at export/serving). These are captured as v2/v3 in the spec's roadmap; keep them out of v1.
